The village

Ahrenshoop, between two waters

A narrow spit of land, the Baltic on one side and the Saaler Bodden on the other. And in between, a village that has drawn painters for more than 130 years.

Ahrenshoop lies on the Fischland, the slimmest part of the Fischland-Darß-Zingst peninsula. From the door it is a few minutes to the Baltic and barely more to the lagoon — two entirely different landscapes, both within an afternoon's walk.

The artists' colony

Why so many people paint here

More than 130 years ago the landscape painter Paul Müller-Kaempff came to Ahrenshoop and stayed, taken by how untouched the place was. Others followed, and the fishing village became an artists' colony.

Galleries, studios and exhibition houses still shape the village today. The art museum, opened in 2013 — five joined pavilions clad in golden brass — documents that history with paintings, prints and sculpture.

Landscape

Cliffs, lagoon, Darß forest

From the Ahrenshoop parish path all the way into neighbouring Wustrow runs the Hohes Ufer — a cliff coast up to 18 metres high, where you can watch the Baltic working visibly at the land.

Eastwards the Darß forest begins, inside the Western Pomerania Lagoon Area National Park; southwards lie the wide lagoon meadows. Because the lagoon shelves so gently, its water warms well — pleasant for children and for watersports alike.

Day trips

What is worth the walk from here

Ahrenshoop art museum

The cultural heart of the village, a few minutes on foot, with the collection on the history of the artists' colony.

The Hohes Ufer

The coastal path towards Wustrow, running along the top of the cliff — the best-known walk in the area.

Darß forest & Weststrand

The national park with its wind-shaped coastal woodland — for many the most striking beach on the German Baltic.

The cycle path to Zingst

Along the peninsula, always between water and wood, as far as Prerow and Zingst.

Lagoon and harbours

Sailing, kiting, excursion boats: the shallow lagoon is the calmer, warmer counterpart to the open sea.

Crane season

In autumn tens of thousands of cranes rest in the lagoon landscape — a reason to come outside the season.
